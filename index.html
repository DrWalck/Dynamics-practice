Setup Lab

Don't solve anything. For each scenario decide: particle or rigid body, which coordinates, where the origin goes, which way the axes point, and which principle. Every choice gets a reason.

Cheat sheet: how to pick

Particle or rigid body?

Ask: does it rotate, and does that rotation matter for what's asked? Forces through G, no ω, no I → particle (even a car or a satellite). Off-center force, ω or α given or asked, moment of inertia in play → rigid body.

Cartesian x–y

Forces keep a constant direction (gravity on a projectile), straight-line motion, or the answer is asked as x and y.

Normal–tangential

The path is known (circle, curve, track). Origin rides on the particle; ê_t along v, ê_n toward the center of curvature.

Polar r–θ

Position described by a distance from a fixed point and an angle, especially when both r and θ change (arms that rotate and extend, orbits, collars on rotating rods). Origin at the pivot.

Rigid-body relative motion

Two points on the same rigid body: v_B = v_A + ω × r_B/A. Frame fixed to the ground; "origin" is the point you know the motion of.

Rotating frame (Coriolis)

Something slides along a body that is itself rotating (pin in a slot, collar on a rotating arm, walking on a turntable). Attach the frame to the rotating body and add 2Ω × v_rel.

Which principle?

Asked for force or acceleration at an instant → Newton/Euler. Speed after a distance with conservative forces → work–energy. Speed after a time or an impact → impulse–momentum. No external force/moment → conservation.
